This was a piece I had great pleasure collaborating on, for several reasons. The first, and most pressing, is that its theme — fusing what is traditional and ancestral in Portuguese culture with modern artistic and rhetorical sensibilities — is something that has inspired this project (Fauxclore) since its roots. As with other Ordem do Ó compositions, there's also a relationship here with the magical and the symbolic.

In the sonoplastia, I tried to build a dichotomy that married traditional Portuguese sounds (cowbells, work songs, goatskin drums) with electronic and "contemporary" experimental music — in quotes, because it sits closer to something that might have come out of the 1990s.

This was perhaps the most cohesive and consistent soundscape I've made, and the one where I started using more recordings of myself playing saxophone — not least because, at its core, it's an instrument that does a fairly good job of emulating bagpipes, an instrument I (sadly) still don't own.

##### Careto

A climactic burst of madness and energy, bringing together almost every piece of music that had appeared throughout the show, with a heavy focus on the rhythm that let the "careto" reach its ecstasy. The progressively heavier distortion, to me, suggests an unravelling of the character's own history, and of tradition itself.

##### Chocalhos

Successive convolution applied to already-convolved audio is a tool I started using after being inspired by Barry Truax, and it's what turned the cowbell recordings of Pedro into near-synthesised drones. In this soundscape I also tried to limit myself to just a few sounds as the starting point for each track, transforming them until they became entirely different instruments.

<audio controls src="/music/ordem_do_o/o_inn_chocalhos.mp3"></audio>

##### Gestos Mágico-Simbólicos

This track is another one shaped by Steve Reich, whose invisible hand runs through almost all of my work. I can never quite escape minimalist influences when asked to write rhythmic, driving music using "traditional" instruments. I also enjoy flirting with orchestral timbre, which here meant leaning on digital instruments alongside a few layers I recorded myself. Someday it might be worth revisiting these pieces recorded by an actual chamber ensemble, but either way I find the current version quite satisfying in tone and expression.

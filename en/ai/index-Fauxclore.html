My thoughts on AI

I would like to make my position on "AI" clear on this page. I will obviously not make it accessible via the website’s links, but in a way I am reassured to know that this page (among others that are equally hidden) exists somewhere on my website, and that someone skilled or resourceful will be able to find it.

I, Jorge Miguel Gonçalves Graça, declare that no LLM or AI was used in the construction of this website, or any of its content. However, it should be noted that I did not sit down at the computer and create the website ex nihilo.

Firstly, I use Eleventy as an engine to generate .html files from a series of templates and .md files. Furthermore, I copied chunks of code almost indiscriminately from:

  1. The Eleventy documentation itself
  2. Posts on Stack Overflow, Stack Exchange, Reddit, forums and various threads.
  3. Source code from pages I visited and liked the style of
  4. MDN Web Docs.

I don’t think this is necessarily the same thing because a) none of the previous elements were the result of indiscriminate theft of other people’s work, and they were shared under open licences for community use; b) I believe that creation always builds on the shoulders of those who came before us, and copying and adapting code is part of the latent culture of the web; and c) in the early 2000s, when I started trying to build websites and learn to code, I followed the same procedure, in a pre-LLM era.

I consider what the major AI corporations (without exception) have done to be an assault on human culture, and whatever advances these technologies may have brought have opened so many doors to forgery, the generation of rubbish, the theft of art, cheap imitation and psychological abuse that I regard them as a kind of crime against humanity. When the dust settles, if we survive until then, only then will we truly realise the potential effects of these technologies.

However, I reject their use, considering that most of their applications are immoral and an attack on creativity and human unity. The promised democratisation of access to creation is a cheap excuse from uncurious souls who have always felt frustrated that the only industries they could not necessarily disrupt were the cultural ones. Anyone who feels that AI has given them the ability to create in a way they were previously unable to do so should first ask themselves whether it was in fact the lack of tools that held them back, or their aversion to the creative act itself. And once they have answered this question, they should ask themselves whether anything has really changed, apart from being able to create facsimiles of facsimiles of facsimiles.

Nothing on this website, now or in the future, will ever be generated by LLMs, and I consider its extraction by any kind of scraper for that purpose to be an obscene act of violation.
